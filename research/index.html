How the five numbers connect

  1. A follow-up step generates 42% of all eventual replies to a cold sequence — nearly half. Stop after the first send and you leave most of that on the table.
  2. But reps don't have time to hand-write a good one. They're already down to ~30% selling time, and adapting a follow-up to what a specific lead did means manually unenrolling them, reading the thread, and rewriting it — one lead at a time.
  3. So sequence tools fire the same generic next step to everyone, regardless of signal — which is a large part of why the average cold email reply rate sits at just 3.43% as generic volume floods every inbox.
  4. And the one signal that used to hint at what to write next — opens — is now unreliable for the 48% of the inbox running Apple Mail, which can fake a 100% open rate regardless of what actually happened.
  5. Reps sending the same fixed step into a colder, more competitive, less-observable inbox is a straight line to average quota attainment sliding to 42.7%, down from 53%.
Where Quota Signal fits

None of this is solved by sending more emails, or by a sequence tool with a better UI. It's solved by reading what actually happened to the last email — bounced, not opened, clicked-no-reply, or genuinely gone quiet — and writing the next one to match, instead of firing the same generic step regardless of signal. That's the entire mechanism, and it's real code today, not a roadmap slide.
